### 1.2 What survives scrutiny

**The paired within-sample delta.** `+44.6% / +47.1% / +46.7%` on dataset B/dataset A/dataset D. This is the
strongest result in the project, for a structural reason: both arms run in one invocation on
one node with one binary, so sample-to-sample variance cancels. Mean 46.13, SD 1.34, t₂ = 4.303
→ **95% CI [+42.8%, +49.5%]**. That interval excludes zero by a wide margin and is
n = 1-per-cell only in the sense that the *pairing* is exact.

The contribution split is internally consistent: `579ad70` gives dataset B 5,813 → 7,949 (+36.7%) for
`ms1_split_valleys`, and 7,949 → 8,411 is +5.8% for `charge:scoring count`;
1.367 × 1.058 = **1.446**, exactly the headline +44.6%. The two defaults are genuinely coupled
and the coupling is documented (`README.md:110-114`).

**The coupling claim itself.** `README.md:110-114` — splitting MS1 traces without `count` drops
charge agreement below baseline and triples the `4→2` confusion — is supported by the charge
table at `README.md:102-108` and is the project's best piece of reasoning. It is the one place
where a lever was evaluated on something other than peptide count and the answer changed the
decision.

**Arithmetic in `charge_im.py`, `funnel.py`, `truthset.py` and `collate.py`.** Re-derived
independently; the code computes what its docstrings say it computes. The defects below are in
*estimand definition* and *interpretation*, not in implementation.

### 1.3 What is shaky

**The 80–93% of the reference implementation headline.** Ratios 93.1 / 80.2 / 86.6: mean 86.63, SD 6.45, t₂ = 4.303
→ **95% CI [70.6%, 102.7%]. The interval includes 100%.** The between-sample spread is 5× wider
than the delta's because the reference implementation's own between-sample variance enters the ratio undamped
(the reference implementation: 9,038 / 11,218 / 11,626, a 29% spread). At n = 3, "80–93% of the reference implementation" and "we
match the reference implementation" are not statistically distinguishable, and neither is "we are at 70%."

Do not quote 80–93% as a range of results; it is a range of *point estimates whose confidence
interval is [70.6, 102.7]*. Quoting the min and max of three points as if they bound the
quantity is the same operation that produced FAILURE 1.

**Charge agreement 74.6% vs a 69.6% constant.** The +5.0 pt margin is real in the sense that the
numbers were computed, but three things degrade it:

- *Population mismatch.* `collate.py:151-152` computes the majority baseline over **all** DIA-NN
  truth precursors (`P`); `collate.py:147` computes the agreement denominator `tot_z` over
  **matched spectra**; `collate.py:163` subtracts them. Two populations *and* two units
  (precursors vs spectra). `collate2.py:1155-1157` names this exact defect as **G-C16** and
  fixes it; `collate2.py` has never run. Both mismatches bias the correct baseline **upward**
  (the matched set is abundance-enriched, hence z = 2-enriched; spectrum-weighting compounds
  it), so **+5.0 pt is an upper bound of unknown tightness.**
- *The treatment contains the control.* `src:499` defaults `assembly:default_charge = 2`;
  `src:1352` stamps z = 2 onto every precursor with no isotope support (`open_search_safe` is a
  `registerFlag_` at `:498`, i.e. off by default). Those precursors enter the agreement
  numerator and denominator as constant-z = 2 predictions. The `guessed` flag exists (`:145`,
  written at `:1352`) and `n_default` is logged (`:1404`), but neither reaches the mzML or the
  metric, so **the reported 74.6% is a mixture of an inference and the constant it is being
  compared against, in an unknown ratio.**
- *The denominator is conditioned on the outcome.* `src:827-848` chooses charge and monoisotope
  **jointly** in one argmax; `best_mono` is the furthest-left partner reached in steps of
  `ISO/z`, so the emitted `mono_mz` is a function of the charge call. `collate.py:112-118`
  admits a spectrum to the confusion matrix only if its precursor m/z matches truth within
  20 ppm. A charge error that also moved the monoisotope shifts m/z by k·1.00335/z — over
  400 ppm at m/z 600 — so it never matches and is **silently excluded from the metric.**
  `charge_agreement` therefore systematically drops a subset of exactly the error class
  `README.md:78-80` says the metric exists to track. Direction: it makes charge look better
  than it is. Magnitude: unmeasured, and not measurable from any committed artifact.

**Truth-set recall.** The in-repo figures are `collate.py:65` and `docs/ms1-funnel.md:48`:
74.3% raw, 35.6% decoy floor, **60.1% corrected**. The decoy is the target m/z + 11.003 Da
(`collate.py:19`), asserted there to be "off the real m/z grid but inside the same isolation
window."

That assertion is false for a large fraction of queries, and the fraction is computable from
the committed window table. `collate.py:106-111` restricts decoy candidates to `[lo, hi]` and
then queries at `pmz + 11.003`; when `pmz + 11.003 > hi` no candidate can match. Voided
fraction per tile = `min(11.003, hi−lo)/(hi−lo)`, over the 24 tiles at `collate.py:171-175`:

| weighting | voided fraction |
|---|---|
| equal weight per window | **34.4%** |
| windows 2–15 (m/z 468–790, the density peak) | **47.1%** |
| range across tiles | 3.8% – 57.0% |
| width-weighted (uniform in m/z) | 19.6% |

Density weighting makes it *worse*, not better — narrow tiles exist because precursor density is
high there, and narrow tiles have the highest void. Width weighting (19.6%) is the only
weighting that squeaks under the project's own guard and is the least defensible one.

`collate2.py:357` sets `SHIFTED_OUT_OF_WINDOW_TOL = 0.20`; `collate2.py:381-387` returns
`CONTROL_ASYMMETRIC` and **refuses to publish** above it (G-C14; `harness/README.md` UNCOVERED
#2). **The published 60.1% breaches the project's own guard by 1.7× to 2.4×.**

Repairing the floor for the voided fraction (the decoy can only fire on the `(1−v)` of queries
that stay in-window, so the true chance rate is `0.356/(1−v)`):

| v | repaired floor | repaired corrected recall |
|---|---|---|
| 0.196 (width-weighted) | 44.3% | **53.9%** |
| 0.344 (equal weight) | 54.3% | **43.8%** |
| 0.471 (dense windows) | 67.3% | 21.4% — and `abbott()` refuses `f > 0.5` |

**60.1% is not recoverable under any weighting.** The honest statement is 44%–54% corrected
recall with a control that the project's own code would refuse to publish. Note this correction
is itself model-dependent: `harness/README.md` UNCOVERED #3 states the Abbott model
("`observed = true + (1−true)·floor`") is "bounds-checked, not justified."

**Signal:interference and purity.** `README.md:60-61` — "2.3× more intensity (5.79% vs 2.52%
net)" and "30% less co-isolation (+7.50% vs +10.70% net)" — is internally consistent
(5.79/2.52 = 2.30; 7.50/10.70 = 0.70) and both `README.md:58` and `BACKLOG.md:42` label it
equal-depth. But `git blame` shows **both lines are from `a9a9b8a`, the initial commit

### 2.8 `69.6%` is used as the chance baseline for two different samples, and in one of them it is the wrong null

`README.md:99-100` uses 69.6% as the **dataset B** majority baseline. `docs/charge-inference.md:26`
runs its ion-mobility test against **dataset A** ground truth and `:36` uses *"a 69.6% chance
baseline."* The same constant to one decimal for two samples, unremarked, in a project whose
`samples.yaml:3-6` preamble reads: *"Every cross-sample error in this project came from a number
whose sample was implicit."*

The second half is worse. `charge-inference.md:36-38` reports: *"75.0% share charge vs a 69.6%
chance baseline — +5.4 points. The plane carries almost no charge information beyond the base
rate. Do not build an IM-based charge prior."*

`scripts/charge_im.py:41-56` computes a **nearest-neighbour share-charge rate**: for each
precursor, does its nearest neighbour in (m/z ± 2 Da, closest IM) have the same charge? For a
share-charge statistic the chance null is **Σ p_z²**, not **max p_z**. `charge_im.py:56` prints
`max(np.bincount(z))/len(z)` — the majority-class rate, which is the null for a *predictor*, not
for an *agreement* rate. With p₂ ≈ 0.696 and a plausible remainder (p₃ ≈ 0.25, p₄ ≈ 0.05),
Σ p_z² ≈ **0.55**.

**The observed margin is therefore ≈ +20 points, not +5.4.** The sentence as written compares an
agreement rate to a predictor's null. This is the sole evidence that falsified the "IM as charge
prior" lever, and it does not support the conclusion it was used to draw.

Two further caveats before treating that lever as reopened rather than settled: the reported
z = 4 mean 1/K₀ (0.966) sitting *above* z = 3 (0.937) contradicts 1/K₀ ∝ CCS/z with CCS ∼
mass^≈0.55; the likeliest explanation is a selection effect — diaPASEF only acquires inside its
mobility polygon, so DIA-NN can only report z = 4 ions that fell inside it, a selection on the
exact variable being tested. `charge-inference.md:22-43` never reports per-cell n (though
`charge_im.py:31` requires n > 20 to print a cell).

**Fix.** Recompute the null as Σ p_z² in `charge_im.py:56`, print the per-cell n and the
polygon coverage, label the sample in `charge-inference.md`, and downgrade "Do not build an
IM-based charge prior" to "not yet tested against the correct null."

### 2.13 The log-overlap scorer's normalisation comment is false

`src:1083-1085` claims unit-sum normalisation makes the score *"1 iff the two normalised elution
shapes agree, invariant to scale."* It does not, whenever the supports differ. `src:1091-1096`
computes `inter = Σ_{k ∈ frag support} min(log1p(f_k)/fsum, log1p(p_k)/plogsum)`, where `fsum`
sums over the fragment's support (`supBegin(fi)..supEnd(fi)`) but `plogsum` (`:1044-1048`)
accumulates over `touched` — the precursor's **full** grid support. Hence
`inter ≤ Σ_{k ∈ frag support} p_k-share < 1` whenever the precursor has signal outside the
fragment's support. Perfect shape agreement over a short fragment support **cannot** reach 1.
This is the same defect class as the second log-sum attempt, with a different denominator, in
the same function.

Separately, `docs/AlphaDIA-and-DIA-BERT.md:44-45` and the repo's own adaptation spec at `:242-245`
specify the log-sum **across candidate fragments per hypothesis**; `src:1097-1100` computes it
for one `(pc, fi)` pair and applies `if (c < min_corr) continue;` — a hard per-fragment
threshold, which is precisely the property the log-sum exists to remove. **The port inverted the
operator's purpose.** This is relevant to §5: the three failures are explained by the port, not
by the idea.

## 3. WEAKNESSES, ranked by threat to open search

Ranked by damage to the stated goal, not by cost to fix. W1–W3 are all more important than every
individual defect in §2.

### W1. The tool is evaluated exclusively on a metric that structurally rewards its worst failure mode, and no goal-aligned metric exists

Every headline — the ratios, the deltas, all six falsified levers — comes from a **closed** Sage
search (`samples.yaml:43`, one pinned config, untracked) scored by `peptide_q`, against a DIA-NN
reference whose space is tryptic / z2–4 / ≤2 mods (`truthset.py:14-16`,
`docs/ms1-funnel.md:88-89` both say so explicitly) and is therefore **structurally incapable of
containing the target class**.

The harness is closed-search-only by construction, not by accident. `bench2.py:1509-1510` pins
`SAGE_REQUIRED_COLUMNS` to a closed set; `collate2.py:768-773` domains the same set;
`collate2.py:144-151` declares no mass-tolerant population; G-C04 pins the header digest so a
delta-mass column cannot appear without an explicit registry edit. `collate2.py:834`:
*"peptide_q is the published metric."*

The bias is not abstract. We emit 4.6 PSMs/peptide vs the reference implementation's 1.46 (`src:508`,
`BACKLOG.md:41`), i.e. 3.2× more independent attempts to clear a peptide-level threshold taken
as a max over PSMs. **Under peptide_q, redundancy is free score.** That single fact predicts, in
advance, everything this project has observed: that every redundancy-reducing lever loses
(competitive, rank-pruning, frame aggregation, `apportion` at −8.9%), that `ms1split` wins while
raising co-isolation 52% and dropping charge agreement below baseline (`f1e1283`), and that
`ae37b29` could delete the disconfirming table and leave a self-consistent README.

**Six falsifications sharing one possibly-biased instrument are one falsification, not six.**

Until an open-search metric exists, this project cannot distinguish "we found more peptides"
from "we bought more lottery tickets," and it has no instrument that would notice if a change
improved open-search performance while lowering peptide_q. That is the definition of optimising
the wrong metric.

### W2. Charge annotation — the quantity the tool's own README says is fatal for open search — is measured by an instrument that excludes the errors it exists to catch, contains its own control, and is calibrated against the wrong baseline

Three independent defects, all documented above (§1.3, §2.8), all pointing the same direction:
**74.6% is optimistic by an unknown amount, and +5.0 pt over baseline is an upper bound.**

The stakes are as the README states them, with one correction. `README.md:78-80`, `src:553`, and
`charge-inference.md:14-20` all assert unconditionally that a z4→z2 error at m/z 600 "fabricates
a −1198 Da phantom modification" that "an open search does not [hide]." Arithmetic:
dM(z4→z2, m/z 600) = **−1197.99 Da**. A one-step charge error gives |dM| = |m/z − 1.007|, which
lands inside a ±500 Da window only for **m/z ≤ 501.0**; a two-step error needs m/z ≤ 251, below
the acquisition range (lowest window 327.5, `truthset.py:29`). **Under MSFragger's standard
−150/+500 or a Sage ±500 wide window, a −1198 Da PSM is not reported at all.**

So the dominant cost of a wrong charge under a realistic open-search window is a **lost
identification**, not a phantom PTM. No file in the repo states the assumed window width. The
conclusion — charge errors are expensive in open search — survives; the mechanism as stated in
three places is wrong, and the fix (a wider window, or reporting them) is different from the fix
implied.

Headroom, for scale: (74.6 − 69.6)/(100 − 69.6) = **16.4%** of the distance to a perfect charge
annotator has been closed. That is the honest framing of "the largest headroom in the tool."

### W3. Not one published number can be reproduced, and the guard machinery that would fix it has never run

§1.1. The severity here is not that numbers are wrong; it is that this project's demonstrated
failure mode is *a right number attached to the wrong arm or the wrong sample*, and the only
defence against that is an artifact chain. `bench2.py` + `collate2.py` are 5.3k lines built
specifically as that defence. They cannot run: `samples.yaml` is v1-shaped and `bench2.py`
aborts G-B00 without `bench_root`. Meanwhile `collate2.py` names and fixes G-C13, G-C14, G-C16 —
three defects that are **live in every published number** — and has never executed.

The project has built the instrument that would catch its own errors and has not connected it.

### W4. The truth-set recall control breaches the project's own asymmetry guard by 1.7–2.4×

§1.3. Published 60.1% is unrecoverable; the honest figure is 44%–54% with a control the code
would refuse. This ranks below W1–W3 because recall against a tryptic/z2–4 reference is a
*proxy* for the wrong population anyway (`ms1-funnel.md:88-89`) — fixing the control makes an
irrelevant number correct.

### W5. Purity is measured but has no delta path, so it cannot influence any decision

`collate2.py:1657-1660` computes paired deltas for exactly three metrics:
`peptides_at_peptide_q`, `recall.corrected`, `charge_agreement`. `own_fraction` and
`coiso_fraction` appear in the per-arm table (`:1638-1639`) but **no delta is ever computed for
them.** Lever decisions are made on deltas. So the two metrics most directly measuring spectrum
*quality* — as opposed to spectrum *count* — are structurally excluded from every decision the
harness supports. This is W1's mechanism made concrete in code.

### W6. Fragment-to-precursor assignment discards the one dimension diaPASEF provides

`src:127-135`: `Trace` stores `double im` as a **scalar centroid** and `xic` as a 1-D
`(rt, intensity)` vector. There is no ion-mobility profile anywhere in the tool.
`scoreCandidates_` (`:1054-1059`) uses IM as a **binary window test** (`pc.im ± delta_im`,
default 0.01) and then computes Pearson over RT alone (`:1099`). A fragment at ΔIM = 0.0099
scores identically to one at ΔIM = 0.0001. Two precursors within 3 s and 0.01 1/K0 are provably
indistinguishable to this scorer — every fragment is emitted into both at full intensity. That
is the mean fan-out of 6.45 (`src:496`, `BACKLOG.md:14`).

diaPASEF's entire premise is that co-eluting precursors separate in mobility. SpeXtractor extracts
one bit per fragment from that dimension. This ranks below W1–W5 because it is an *opportunity*,
not an error — but it is the precondition for §5's decomposition and ML options, and it is why
the log-sum port could never have worked (§2.13, §5.1).

### W7. Known-defective code paths remain default-on or default-off against their own documentation

`charge:iso_im_tolerance` 0.05 (§2.4). `assembly:open_search_safe` off (§2.6).
`assembly:default_charge` 2 (§1.3). Each individually small; together they mean the shipped
configuration is not the one the code's own comments argue for.

### W8. `docs/BACKLOG.md`'s next-item justification contains the project's signature error

`BACKLOG.md:26-31`: *"the number of components is not estimated, it is observed. MS1 tells us
which precursors are present."* Against `docs/ms1-funnel.md:2`: **2,521,685 MS1 traces →
1,206,174 precursor hypotheses** against ~43,499 real DIA-NN precursors. The design matrix would
be ~96% spurious columns by count. MS1 tells us where 1.2M *hypotheses* are; that is not the
same as knowing the basis, and it is the load-bearing justification for the entire item. Right
observation, wrong interpretation, no control — live in the backlog as the next thing to build.

Second defect in the same item: `BACKLOG.md:36-37` and `:71` state `assembly:apportion` is
"currently **untested**, so measure it first" and set it as the prerequisite. It has been tested:
**−8.9%**, listed as falsified in the brief. The prerequisite the backlog specified has already
returned a negative that nobody propagated.

## 4. THE BOTTLENECK

The binding constraint is **the absence of a measurement that points at the goal.** Not charge,
not co-elution scoring, not model capacity, not compute. SpeXtractor exists to find things a
library-based tool cannot report, and it is steered entirely by a closed-search peptide count
against a library-derived reference — an instrument that (a) cannot contain the target class by
construction, (b) rewards emitting more spectra per peptide, which is exactly the mechanism the
project's own commit `f1e1283` identified as what its winning default actually does, and (c) has
already, six times, returned "falsified" for every lever that would reduce redundancy. Every
downstream question is unanswerable until this is fixed: whether split_count is discovery or
inflation, whether charge inference is worth improving, whether NNLS or a model would help, and
whether the tool is at 80% or 100% of the reference implementation on the axis anyone should care about. The
project is not short of ideas, compute, or discipline — it has produced two voluntary
retractions in a week, which is more rigour than most — it is short of an instrument, and it has
been spending that discipline auditing numbers produced by the wrong one.

## 5. OPTIONS ASSESSED

### 5.1 Transformer

**Verdict: not justified. Two preconditions fail before architecture is the question.**

*Precondition 1 — the mobility dimension is not in the data structure.* W6. There is no RT×IM
grid for a 2-D operator to act on. This also explains the three failed log-sum ports at −9% to
−68%: AlphaDIA's log-sum collapses over an RT×IM grid where co-elution is enforced by
construction; ours collapses over RT alone, which is exactly the axis on which co-eluting
precursors are degenerate. **A 2-D operator was ported onto 1-D data.** Compounding it, the port
was applied per-pair as a hard gate rather than per-hypothesis across fragments (§2.13),
inverting the property the statistic exists to provide. The idea was not wrong; the substrate
and the adaptation both were. No model can learn from a dimension the pipeline discards.

*Precondition 2 — there is no valid estimate of what the target is worth.* The only
quantification of the charge problem is the retracted funnel. And the project's own code says
the closed benchmark cannot see charge: `src:498` cites "charge unset cost only 1.3%:
8,123→8,019." (Caveat, stated because it matters: that figure has **no provenance anywhere** —
`git grep 8123` returns only the help string — and the experiment it describes is
`default_charge 2 → 0`, which touches only isotope-unsupported precursors, not charge annotation
as a whole. Do not quote 1.3% as an established bound. The *mechanism* — closed search
enumerates charge, so peptide_q under-registers charge error — is the project's own position at
`src:498` and `:553` and survives independently.)

*What a transformer would concretely buy over an MLP:* one thing — permutation-invariant
handling of a variable-size fragment set, weighting each fragment individually and conditioning
its contribution on the others. That is the right inductive bias for chimeric deconvolution. It
is realisable at **10⁴–10⁵ parameters** via DeepSets (Zaheer et al., NeurIPS 2017) or single-head
attention pooling (Lee et al., Set Transformer, ICML 2019). It does not require 8 encoder blocks
or 276M labels. Everything else transformers offer — long-range sequence modelling, pretraining
transfer, emergent scale behaviour — has no referent: there is no sequence at the point of
decision, and the physics a large model would have to learn (isotope spacing 1.00335/z,
averagine ratios, CCS–mass relations) is analytically specified.

*Labels.* ~130k gross is much softer than it looks: `samples.yaml:10,20,28` show dataset A/dataset B/dataset D are
all from `the benchmark cohort` — one tissue, one instrument, one method, one gradient.
That is ~50–70k *unique* precursors observed up to three times. Cross-sample validation across
them measures run-to-run reproducibility, not generalisation. Against a from-scratch transformer
requirement of ~10⁶ precursors / ≥100 runs / ≥3 tissues: **~8× short on labels, ~30× short on
diversity.**

*A trap to pre-register before anyone trains anything.* At typical tryptic distributions z = 4 is
~4–5% of DIA-NN precursors — ~3,000 unique against ~30,000 z = 2. **Any accuracy-minimising model
learns never to predict z = 4.** That recovers most of the gap toward 69.6% and looks like
success, while destroying detection of exactly the large multiply-charged precursors most likely
to carry unexpected modifications. Optimising charge *accuracy* would make the number go up and
the tool worse. The correct objective is a **calibrated posterior with an abstain option**, not
accuracy — and `assembly:open_search_safe` already implements abstention at zero training cost
and is default-off (§2.6).

*Also relevant:* Grinsztajn et al. (NeurIPS 2022) and Shwartz-Ziv & Armon (*Information Fusion*
2022) both find tree ensembles beat deep nets on tabular data at n ≤ 10⁵ — AlphaDIA's 46 features
at n ≈ 10⁵ is that regime. Deep learning has paid off in proteomics as a **physics emulator from
sequence** (Prosit, *Nat Methods* 2019; AlphaPeptDeep, *Nat Commun* 2022) while the rescorer
stayed small (Percolator, *Nat Methods* 2007; DIA-NN's small feed-forward ensemble, *Nat Methods*
2020). SpeXtractor has no sequence at the point of decision, so the one pattern that reliably wins
is unavailable to it. DIA-BERT is the counterexample and its enabling conditions are exactly what
we lack: the library fragments *are* the query, plus 276M labels.

**Cost if pursued anyway:** 2–4 weeks engineering for the RT×IM retention (prerequisite), then
4–8 weeks model work, GPU time available but not the constraint.

**Falsification criterion (pre-register):** *If a charge oracle — overwrite SpeXtractor's charge
with DIA-NN truth wherever a match exists, re-run — yields <3% additional peptides at peptide_q
**and** the sloped-ray fraction in an open-search delta-mass histogram is <5% of PSMs, then
charge inference is not the bottleneck and every ML proposal targeting it is abandoned.*

**P(a transformer beats the hand-crafted path on a goal-aligned metric within 6 months at
current label scale): ~8%.** Dominant failure mode is not architecture — it is that the
goal-aligned metric does not yet exist, so "beats" is undefined.

### 5.2 Decomposition — SVD / NMF / PARAFAC

**Verdict: do not build any of them. The prior reasoning is right about global SVD for a weaker
reason than the real one, right about local non-negative decomposition, and wrong about
PARAFAC.**

*Global SVD.* "Not low-rank" is the soft version. The hard version is arithmetic: a window's
data matrix over 30 cycles is (fragment m/z bins × 30), so its rank is bounded by **30**
regardless of chemistry, while the component count is 56–800 (below). The matrix is *truncated
below the component count*; any rank-r ≤ 30 factorisation is forced to merge components and
noise decides which. Use that framing; it is not arguable. Orthogonality being wrong for
non-negative overlapping components is also correct and standard.

*Actual rank.* From committed numbers: 33,553 spectra, 1,343 MS1 (`src:402`) → 32,210 MS2 / 1,343
cycles = **24.0 windows**, one sampling per cycle. Elution peaks span 4–22 cycles (`src:212`),
median w ≈ 12. From dataset A totals (43,499 DIA-NN precursors, `ms1-funnel.md:2`):

- concurrent at one instant, per window = 43,499 × 12 / 1,343 / 24 = **16.2**
- distinct precursors overlapping a 30-cycle span = 43,499 × 42 / 1,343 / 24 = **56.7**

Note: the frequently-quoted "~50 co-isolated precursors per spectrum" is a misreading — that is
DIA-NN's `RT.Start`→`RT.Stop` *integration* window (which `truthset.py` uses to build density),
not the chromatographic peak. It is the 30-cycle-span number, not a concurrent count. Do not
stack the two.

Loaded windows (m/z 400–700, where tiles are 19–33 Da) carry several times the mean: **150–200
DIA-NN-visible precursors per 30-cycle span.** And DIA-NN-visible is the minority: the truth-set
assay attributes only ~13–23% of pseudo-spectrum intensity to any DIA-NN-known precursor, and
DIA-NN's space is tryptic/z2–4/≤2 mods, so it is structurally blind to the rest. Since
unattributed species are individually weaker, the count multiplier exceeds the intensity ratio.

| | sparse window | loaded window |
|---|---|---|
| DIA-NN-visible, 30-cycle span | 40–60 | 150–200 |
| **R_true (all species)** | **~80–300** | **~300–800** |
| matrix columns available | 30 | 30 |
| **R after a ±0.01 1/K0 slab** | **~3–10** | **~10–25** |

*PARAFAC — the premise is right, the application fails on two independent grounds.*

(a) **Kruskal's condition cannot be satisfied.** Uniqueness needs k_A + k_B + k_C ≥ 2R + 2
(Kruskal, *Lin Alg Appl* 1977). Mode A (fragment m/z) can grow with R; **modes B and C are
dimension-capped by physics and do not scale with R** — 30 cycles is 30 cycles, and the
transmitted mobility band is fixed by the method. At R = 50 the requirement is 102 against an
*optimistic* budget of ~115 (marginal, and same-width Gaussian families do not have full
numerical k-rank); at R = 100 the requirement is 202 against ~165. It fails, and the deficit
widens monotonically as the window gets more crowded — i.e. worst exactly where deconvolution is
needed.

(b) **The IM mode is a partition, not a profile, and this is decisive.** In PASEF, TIMS
separation happens *before* the collision cell — the tool's own header states this at `src:49-51`.
A fragment's recorded 1/K0 is therefore not a property of the fragment; it is an index of its
precursor's arrival time. Every fragment of precursor r sits at one 1/K0 value μ_r, broadened
only by fixed instrument resolution. Mode C for component r is a same-width Gaussian differing
from every other component **only in centre** — a translation family, i.e. a categorical label,
not a resolving dimension. A third mode buys identifiability when components have
overlapping-but-*differently-shaped* profiles in it. Here it partitions into slabs and leaves 2-D
MCR-ALS inside each slab with the rotational ambiguity intact. **PARAFAC's marginal contribution
over what `gate:delta_im` already does is exactly zero on the partition, plus a method already
rejected inside it.** "diaPASEF's IM supplies a third mode most DIA lacks" is true about the data
and false about its consequence.

(c) PARAFAC2 is not a rescue: it relaxes one mode's invariance (Kiers, ten Berge & Bro, *J
Chemometrics* 1999), reintroducing the rotational freedom trilinearity was supposed to remove.

*Identifiability summary.* Window-level 2-D: no (R_true 80–800 vs 30 columns). Window-level
PARAFAC: no (Kruskal + partition). Slab-level blind: no (rank selection is still the unknown).
**Slab-level with a correct basis: yes** — 30 equations, ~10 non-negative unknowns,
overdetermined; two Gaussians of width σ separated by Δ have ρ = exp(−Δ²/4σ²), and 5–10
precursors over 30 cycles gives ρ̄ ≈ 0.7–0.89, κ ≈ 13–41. Workable. **Contrary to
`BACKLOG.md:52-58`, ill-conditioning is not the binding constraint.** *Slab-level with **our**
basis: no* — ~10 real columns among 100–500 candidates against 30 equations (W8). NNLS returns a
sparse solution, but there are combinatorially many equally-good ones and it picks one
confidently, which is worse than honest sharing because downstream it looks decisive.

*Field evidence.* Every member of this family that works in DIA gets its basis from an enumerated
library: Specter (Peckner et al., *Nat Methods* 2018) does exactly this and requires a spectral
library; CHIMERYS performs non-negative unmixing against predicted-library bases (*moderate
confidence on that exact citation — verify before quoting*). The blind version has been available
for fifteen years and no production DIA tool uses it — DIA-Umpire, DIA-NN, Spectronaut, AlphaDIA
and the reference implementation all use correlation and scoring. AlphaDIA, with far more resources, explicitly
*suppresses* chimeras (winner-take-all at ≥3 shared fragments,
`docs/AlphaDIA-and-DIA-BERT.md:114-122`) rather than deconvolving them. That is not an oversight
in five independent groups. And SpeXtractor's reason to exist is not having a library
(`README.md:6-8`).

*The one thing worth taking from the steel-man:* stop using IM to partition and **vectorise
(RT × IM) jointly as the NNLS response**. 30 cycles × ~10 IM scans = 300 nominal equations
instead of 30. Discount honestly — the ~10 IM rows within a slab are same-width Gaussians
differing by less than one FWHM and are ~90% redundant, so effective equations go to perhaps
70–90, not 300. That upgrades a hopeless problem to a marginal one. It is a change to how `P` is
built, not a new algorithm, and it costs nothing extra once the oracle harness exists.

**Cost:** the three diagnostics below are **~5 person-days, zero GPU, minutes of wall clock, no
C++ changes.** Building SVD/NMF/PARAFAC properly would be 4–8 weeks.

**Falsification criteria (pre-register all three):**
- **T1 — singular-value spectrum with a time-shuffled control.** One loaded window (327.5–468.8)
  and one sparse window over 30 cycles, read from `.d` via alphatims (~50 lines). Report
  components to reach 90%/99% Frobenius energy and effective rank exp(H(σ²)) (Roy & Vetterli,
  EUSIPCO 2007). Then repeat with cycle order permuted independently per m/z row. *Kill:* 99%
  energy needs >25 of 30 components, **or** real-data effective rank is within 20% of the
  shuffled control. **The shuffled control is the part that must not be skipped — its absence is
  precisely what produced the funnel retraction.**
- **T2 — conditioning within an IM slab, oracle basis vs ours.** Build `P` from (i) MS1 XICs of
  DIA-NN-asserted precursors only, (ii) our `diag:dump_ms1_tsv` hypotheses. Report median κ(P)
  and **column count**. The diagnostic is the ratio of column counts, not κ.
- **T3 — oracle NNLS (the one worth paying for).** Run the assignment-precision test at
  `BACKLOG.md:44-49` (405,336 DIA-NN precursor–fragment pairs; negatives = fragments of other
  co-eluting precursors in the same window), comparing pairwise Pearson vs `apportion` vs NNLS
  **given the true precursor list**. ~10⁶ solves at 30×15 ≈ 50 µs ≈ one minute single-core.
  *Kill:* oracle-NNLS does not beat pairwise Pearson on assignment precision → **no method in
  this class can help; close the file permanently.** If it wins, the oracle-minus-ours gap
  directly measures how much of the loss is hypothesis quality rather than assignment.

**Explicit non-recommendation:** do not run PARAFAC "just to see." If pressure survives, the
minimum honest version is CORCONDIA (Bro & Kiers, *J Chemometrics* 2003) at R = 2…20 on one slab;
expect <30% core consistency at R > 3 and two-factor degeneracies at R > 5. That is a 3-day
answer to a question T1 + T2 answer in two.

**Probabilities:**

| claim | P |
|---|---|
| Any of SVD / NMF / PARAFAC yields >2% peptide_q gain over current defaults | **<5%** |
| PARAFAC survives CORCONDIA at R ≥ 5 on a real slab | **<10%** |
| T1's real-data effective rank meaningfully above its shuffled control | ~25% |
| Oracle-NNLS beats pairwise Pearson on assignment precision (T3) | **~40%** |
| …and that converts to >2% peptide_q | ~15% conditional (~6% unconditional) |
| NNLS on **our** 1.2M-hypothesis basis beats pairwise Pearson | **~10%** |

The <5% is not reflexive pessimism. It rests on four independent things: the rank arithmetic
above, the Kruskal budget, the fifteen-year negative field result, and this project's own
measured −8.9% on `assembly:apportion`, which is the same idea in its crudest form. **Note the
gap between the last two rows — that gap is the finding: the unmixing algorithm is not the
constraint, the basis is.**

### 5.3 No ML — build the open-search benchmark

**Verdict: this is the recommendation. It is the only option that addresses §4.**

*Why no ML, in order of decisiveness.* (1) **The labels are anti-correlated with the objective.**
130k DIA-NN precursors, all canonical tryptic z2–4 ≤2-mod. A supervised scorer trained on them
learns "resembles a peptide DIA-NN already found"; applied to a blind-search extractor its
optimal behaviour is to *suppress* the non-canonical spectra the tool exists to produce. That is
label–target misalignment, and no compute budget repairs it. (2) **Circularity closes the escape.**
DIA-BERT's protocol works because positives are the intersection of two *independent* tools;
`docs/AlphaDIA-and-DIA-BERT.md:201-204` already notes Sage-on-our-pseudospectra is not
independent of our extraction, so the second vote must be DIA-NN — the same closed space.
(3) **Wrong stage.** Both reviewed architectures are *rescorers*; SpeXtractor's defect is upstream,
in candidate construction (fan-out 6.45) and annotation correctness. A rescorer cannot repair a
spectrum whose fragment content is wrong; it can only decline to report it. (4) **The SOTA in
this data type declines the transformer** — AlphaDIA is 10,810 parameters over hand-crafted
features, CPU-capable, and its strongest defensible claim is FDR *calibration*, not ID count.
(5) **Process risk**, which is the argument to lead with in a budget review: eleven data-handling
errors and two retractions in one week, all of one shape — right number, wrong interpretation,
control checked afterwards. ML is the least interpretable, hardest-to-falsify intervention
available, and its characteristic failure (memorising the label generator) is *indistinguishable
from success on the current metric*. Adding a model to a system whose error-detection is the
bottleneck adds an error source to the wrong side of the ledger.

*What to build instead.* An entrapment-controlled open-search arm.

1. **Open arm in the harness:** same spectra, same FASTA, Sage with precursor tolerance
   ≈ −150/+500 Da, zero variable mods so all mass shift lands in the open dimension.
   **Version-control the Sage config in the repo** — `sage_deiso.json` currently lives on
   `/scratch` and is untracked, a reproducibility hole in the single file that defines the metric.
2. **Entrapment FASTA:** append a foreign proteome (e.g. *A. thaliana*) at a known ratio.
   Entrapment gives an empirical FDR independent of the decoy model, which is the accepted way to
   validate FDR when the search space changes (Wen, Freestone, Noble, Käll, Nesvizhskii et al.,
   *Nat Methods* 2025). This is necessary because open search grows the candidate space ~10³× and
   decoy-based FDR degrades accordingly (Kong et al., *Nat Methods* 2017; Chick et al., *Nat
   Biotechnol* 2015).
3. **Metric:** PSMs at ≤1% *entrapment-estimated* FDR with |Δmass| > 0.1 Da, plus unique modified
   peptides, plus PSMs/peptide.
4. **The free diagnostic — this is the part that retires the retracted assay.** The delta-mass
   histogram. A correct tool produces sharp peaks at +15.9949, +42.0106, +0.984, −17.0265. A tool
   with broken charge produces density along **rays through (1.00728, 0) with integer slopes**,
   because ΔM = (z′−z)(m/z − 1.00728) is *linear in m/z*, while real modifications are
   **horizontal bands** at constant ΔM. The two are trivially separable in the 2-D (precursor
   m/z, Δmass) histogram, **with no ground truth and no decoy floor.** The sloped-ray mass
   fraction is a direct measurement of charge error *in the actual use case*, and it is strictly
   better than the DIA-NN-anchored proxy. Choose the window width so the rays are visible
   (§3/W2: at ±500 Da a −1198 shift is not reported at all — this is a design parameter of the
   experiment, and it must be stated in the config).
5. **`assembly:open_search_safe` ON in the open arm.** It already implements the correct
   behaviour (`src:498`). It is off because turning it on costs closed-search count. For a tool
   whose README states its purpose is open search, that cost is the right answer being scored by
   the wrong instrument.
